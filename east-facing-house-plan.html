East facing house plan: entrance, rooms and Vastu

An east facing plot has its road on the east side. It is one of the most sought-after facings in India, partly for Vastu and partly for climate: the front of the house gets the gentle morning sun and is in shade by afternoon.

Room placement for an east facing house

These are the conventional Vastu placements most families ask for:

  • Main entrance: on the east side, towards the north-east rather than the south-east.
  • Living room: north-east or east, at the front.
  • Kitchen: south-east, with the cook facing east. North-west is the usual alternative.
  • Master bedroom: south-west, at the rear.
  • Pooja room: north-east.
  • Staircase: south, west or south-west.
  • Toilets: west or north-west; never in the north-east or the centre.
  • Centre: kept open or lightly used.

Sun and comfort

The east front gets soft morning light, so living rooms and verandahs at the front are pleasant through the day. The rear (west) wall takes the hottest afternoon sun. Keep west windows small, put sunshades or a jaali on them, and place wardrobes, stores or bathrooms along that wall as a buffer for the bedrooms.

Common mistakes

Entrance in the south-east corner. It often happens when the car porch takes the north-east. Plan the porch and the door together.

Following Vastu and ignoring light. A south-west master bedroom with only west windows gets hot and dark. Add a north or south window wherever you can.

Questions

Frequently asked

Which corner should the main door be in for an east facing house?

Conventionally towards the north-east part of the east side, avoiding the south-east end.

Where should the kitchen be in an east facing house?

South-east is preferred, with the cooking platform placed so you face east. North-west is the common alternative.

Is an east facing house good for Indian weather?

Generally yes. The front gets morning sun and is shaded in the afternoon; the west wall at the back needs shading.
